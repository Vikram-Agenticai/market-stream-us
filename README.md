"ShelVey's advanced AI automation technology represents a transformational opportunity for Kenect," said Shaun Sorensen, the chief executive and co-founder of Kenect, in an interview. The deal comes at a moment when businesses across sectors are racing to integrate AI capabilities into their operations, driven by advances in large language models and automation technology. According to a recent report by [McKinsey & Company](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai), companies that have adopted AI report productivity gains of up to 40 percent in certain business functions.

A Strategic Bet on AI

For Kenect, which was founded by Mr. Sorensen and has grown to serve thousands of businesses across North America, the acquisition of ShelVey marks its most significant expansion into artificial intelligence. The company's existing platform allows businesses to send and receive text messages with customers — a service that has become increasingly important as consumers show a preference for text-based communication over phone calls.

But as competitors enter the market and customer expectations evolve, Kenect executives believe AI automation will be critical to staying ahead.

ShelVey, a Delaware limited liability company formed in November 2024, has developed what its founders describe as enterprise-grade AI automation technology. The platform uses proprietary algorithms to handle complex customer interactions, process data, and execute tasks that would typically require human intervention.

"We are excited to join forces with Kenect and integrate our AI automation capabilities into their established platform," said Vikram Varma Sangaraju, the director of ShelVey. What Kenect Is Buying

The deal also includes all domain registrations, technical documentation, and existing customer contracts.

Industry analysts say the valuation reflects both the strategic importance of AI capabilities and the premium that established companies are willing to pay to acquire talent and technology in the field.

"We're seeing a pattern where traditional software companies are buying AI startups not just for the technology, but for the expertise," said Thomas Chen, a technology analyst at [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/information-technology). "The knowledge of how to build and deploy these systems is as valuable as the systems themselves."

"The hard part isn't buying the technology — it's actually making it work with your existing systems and delivering value to customers," said Rebecca Martinez, a venture capital investor who focuses on enterprise software. For Kenect, it's a bet that AI will fundamentally transform business communication — and that companies that move quickly to integrate these capabilities will have a significant advantage over those that wait.
